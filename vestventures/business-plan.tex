\section{Executive summary}\label{sec:rezumat}\nobreak
% =====================================================================

\textbf{What we do.} ADMINISTRATIVO is a multi-tenant web application that runs the
employer side of HR and administration for a Romanian small or medium company: time
tracking from the employee's phone, leave, personnel files, filing with
\textbf{REGES-Online} (the Labour Inspectorate's mandatory electronic employee register)
through its official API, occupational health and safety (\textbf{SSM}), onboarding,
courses, performance reviews and KPIs, fleet, maintenance, inventory, IT ticketing,
announcements, an employee portal and an AI help assistant. Payroll is built and is
undergoing validation by an accountant. Customers switch on only the modules they need and
pay a flat monthly price per company.

\textbf{Why now.} Since 1 January 2026, REGES-Online is the only valid employee register in
Romania. Every hire, salary change, suspension and termination must be filed there, mostly
within days; late or missing filing is fined up to 8,000 RON per person\src{10}. When the
deadline was extended in September 2025, only 23\% of employers had migrated\src{8}. About
526,000 Romanian companies employ at least one person\src{1}. We expect that many of them still
keep attendance, leave and HR files on paper or in Excel; no survey we know of measures this.

\textbf{Where we are.} Development started on 17 August 2026. The product has been live at
administrativo.ro since early September 2026: 19 modules, 811 commits, 162 database migrations,
and tenant isolation enforced by the database itself (row-level security forced on 160 of 164
tables). Transmission to REGES-Online through the API was confirmed working in production, for a
real company, on 17 September 2026. We have \textbf{zero paying customers}; demo companies show
the product to prospects. On 6 October 2026 we opened a pilot with up to 10 places for
accounting practices (sign-up until 15 November 2026; none signed yet, two qualified conversations).

\textbf{How we will sell.} Accountants are the \emph{strategic} channel: one practice can bring
several client companies, and we expect employers to trust a tool their accountant recommends.
In the base model the channel grows from 29\% of new paying companies in year~1 to 45\% in
year~3: about 233 of 591 over 36 months. Direct trials from search, free tools and paid ads bring
the rest. Their cost per trial and conversion are untested assumptions (Section~\ref{sec:financiar}).

\textbf{The ask.} A €\cf{round_eur} pre-seed round at €\cfm{pre_money_eur}M pre-money. It pays
every planned cost for \cf{runway_months} months with zero revenue, and on the base plan it carries
the company to monthly break-even in \cf{base.breakeven_date} (month \cf{base.breakeven_month}) without
further funding. It funds a deliberately lean plan: both co-founders full time and unpaid in
year~1 (€1,000 gross each from month 13), a partner
manager for the accountant channel (half time from month 3, full time from month 13) and a
half-time onboarding specialist from month 9. The developer is hired only after the seed. The
goal is 100 paying companies by \cf{base.date_to_100} (month \cf{base.months_to_100}), when we
raise a seed round to grow faster, not to survive. Beyond the model, our ambition for 2031 is
\cf{ambition_2031.paying_customers} paying companies and about €\cfm{ambition_2031.arr_eur}M ARR,
\cfn{ambition_2031.share_of_sam_pct}\% of our serviceable market (Section~\ref{sec:ambitie}).

\section{Problem and regulatory context}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{The problem}

A Romanian employer with 5 to 50 people carries the same legal paperwork as a large company,
usually without an HR department. Our working hypothesis, not yet measured: the office manager
or the owner keeps the daily attendance record, approves leave, tracks medical checks and safety
briefings, watches vehicle inspection dates and sends personnel changes to the accountant, who
may file them with the Labour Inspectorate; much of it lives in paper registers, Excel files and
messaging apps. Mistakes surface when an inspector visits, and late or missing REGES filings are
fined per employee\src{10}.

\textbf{Risk to the thesis, disclosed.} On 2 April 2026 the Constanța Court of Appeal annulled HG
295/2025 at first instance (Decision 83/2026). The ruling is not final; the request to suspend the
HG was rejected, so REGES-Online remains applicable while the appeal is pending at the High Court
of Cassation and Justice\src{12}. No final ruling was found as of 6 October 2026. The duties to
keep an employee register (art.~34) and a daily working-time record (art.~119) come from the
Labour Code and do not depend on that case.

\textbf{Tailwind, not yet law.} The EU Pay Transparency Directive (2023/970) had to be transposed
by 7 June 2026. Romania has a draft law (30 March 2026) still in Parliament; pay-gap reporting
targets employers with 100+ staff\src{13}. We treat it as a future reason to keep pay data
structured, not as a current driver.

\subsection{Architecture and security}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Tenant isolation in the database, not only in the application.} Postgres row-level
  security is enabled on all 164 public tables and \emph{forced} on 160 of them (the other four are
  global catalogues and identity tables), with 443 policies. Isolation between client companies is
  tested by SQL probes (30 files) that check both that nobody sees another company's data and that
  each role can still write what it should.
\item \textbf{Encrypted sensitive data.} Personal numeric codes (CNP) and bank accounts (IBAN) are
  stored with AES-256-GCM, a random IV per value and key versioning. Reading them requires an
  explicit permission, and decryption for payroll exports leaves an audit row.
\item \textbf{Append-only audit log}, enforced by a database trigger even for the service role.
\item \textbf{Five roles} (platform admin, company admin, manager, HR, employee) with scoped
  permissions (none / own / team / all); every page and every server action checks them.
\item \textbf{Legal values are data, not code}: payroll, leave, per-diem and working-time values
  live in dated, versioned tables, so a change in law is a settings change.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{REGES-Online over the API}\label{sec:reges}\nobreak

The REGES module composes the legally required events from the employee record (hire, salary,
job, working-time and duration changes, suspension and resumption, secondment, termination,
corrections) and queues them with their dependencies. It authenticates with each employer's
own API credentials, stored encrypted and readable only by the server. Messages that carry an
employee's personal numeric code are sent by a person with transmit rights; network errors are
retried, rejected messages are not. Receipts and answers are reconciled back onto the employee
record. Transmission through the production API was confirmed working on 17 September 2026.
Each customer must first obtain API access from the REGES portal.

\section{Go-to-market}\label{sec:gtm}\nobreak
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Channel 1: accountants (strategic)}

Our hypothesis, to be tested in the pilot: most small employers already work with an accountant,
and many accountants handle their clients' labour paperwork, including REGES filings.
ADMINISTRATIVO would take the daily HR work off both of them: the employer records attendance,
leave and changes; the accountant logs in with one account across clients and takes the exports.
We call the channel strategic, not dominant: in the base model it brings 33\% of new paying
companies over 36 months (Section~\ref{sec:canale}), rising from 24\% in year~1 to 38\% in year~3.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pilot (opened 6 Oct 2026):} 10 places for accounting practices, sign-up until 15 Nov
  2026, 1--3 client companies each, free until 31 Mar 2027. The team imports employees and sets up
  each company. Payroll enters the pilot only after the accountant validates the legal values. We
  ask for a 30-minute monthly feedback call and consent to an anonymised case description. As of
  6 Oct 2026 no practice has signed yet.
\item \textbf{After the pilot:} a partner and sales manager (half time from month 3, full time
  from month 13) signs practices. Model assumptions (base): a full-time manager signs 2.5 new partners
  a month in year~1, 4 in year~2 and 5 in year~3, and signings scale with the manager's hours, so
  year~1 runs at half that pace; each active partner refers one company every four months; 55\% of
  referrals convert. That gives 19 / 67 / 127 active partners at months 12 / 24 / 36 (\proj), under
  1\% of the ≈18,400 practices.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Channel 2: search and free tools}

administrativo.ro offers 7 free tools without an account (salary calculator, leave request form,
attendance register, review form, SSM briefing sheet, vehicle trip sheet, timesheet), 8 legal guides
(including REGES-Online and working-time records), 19 module pages, 4 industry pages and a page for
accountants. Search traffic has just started (Section~\ref{sec:tractiune}). Model assumption: 6 organic trials a month
in year~1, 16 in year~2, 27 in year~3; 27.5\% of trials convert after the free month (\proj;
\textbf{untested}: no trial has been run yet).

\subsection{Channel 3: direct and paid}

Founder-led demos (\fisier{/cere-demo}), LinkedIn and paid search on REGES-Online and timesheet
keywords. The marketing budget grows from €1,500 to €3,500 a month over three years; 60\% of it
goes to performance ads at an assumed €70 per trial (\proj). Both the €70 per trial and the
27.5\% trial conversion are \textbf{untested assumptions}: paid campaigns have not run yet. Paid
conversions start in model month~2, and over 36 months the base case's 771 paid trials bring
about 200 new paying companies, close to the accountant channel's about 233. This is the model's main sensitivity
(Section~\ref{sec:riscuri}).

\subsection{Sequencing}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item Turn the pilot into evidence: signed pilot agreements, usage data, conversion on 1 April 2027.
\item Sell a narrow, legally safe core first (attendance, leave, REGES, SSM, employee portal);
  other modules are upsell.
\item Start selling payroll only after the accountant's validation (planned for month 5).
\item Region first (Timiș and the West Region), then national. Expansion to neighbouring markets is a
  hypothesis for after the seed round (Section~\ref{sec:international}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{International expansion (hypothesis)}\label{sec:international}\nobreak

Nothing in this round's numbers pays for a second country. We state the ambition and how we would
test it.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Candidate markets:} Moldova, Serbia, Bulgaria and Hungary, the neighbouring markets
  whose teams Vest Ventures already accepts\src{27}. Each has its own labour-law registers and
  payroll rules; we have not yet studied them.
\item \textbf{What carries over as built:} the multi-tenant core with database-enforced isolation,
  legal values stored as dated settings data rather than code, attendance and clock-in, leave,
  personnel files, the structure of the health-and-safety, fleet, maintenance and inventory
  registers, onboarding, courses, reviews, ticketing and the employee portal.
\item \textbf{What is country-specific:} the REGES-Online integration, payroll rules and statutory
  returns (such as D112), national leave and safety rules, and the interface language (Romanian
  only today).
\item \textbf{Trigger and milestone:} a market scan of one neighbouring country after the seed round
  closes (after month \cf{base.months_to_100} in the base case), funded by the seed, at zero cost in
  this round.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Regulatory}: HG 295/2025 was annulled at first instance (2 Apr 2026, not final); REGES rules or
deadlines could change & The register (Labour Code art.~34) and daily timesheet (art.~119) are statutory
and do not depend on the case. REGES is one module, not the whole product. Legal values live in
versioned settings tables, so rule changes are configuration. \\
\textbf{Payroll validation}: every payroll legal value is still marked ``to be confirmed''; one known
calculation defect is open & Payroll is not sold until an accountant validates it (budgeted, month 5).
The Finance package is only 5\% of the plan mix. The pilot runs without payroll. \\
\textbf{No market validation yet}: zero paying customers, no signed pilot or letter of intent; the
accountant channel is untested & Pilot with up to 10 practices (sign-up until 15 Nov 2026; 0 signed on
6 Oct, two qualified conversations; base case assumes 8, conservative 6); pilot conversion measured on 1 April 2027; the model's assumptions are
explicit inputs and the conservative case is shown. \\
\textbf{Acquisition assumptions}: most modelled new companies come from direct trials; paid trials at
€70 each converting at 27.5\% have never been tested; paid conversions start in model month~2 & This is
the model's key sensitivity. Paid budget is spent in small, measured steps from month 1; cost per trial
and conversion are named inputs and are replaced by measured values after the first campaigns. If
paid trials cost more or convert less, the paid budget is cut and the plan leans on the accountant
channel and search, which moves the base case towards the conservative case. \\
\textbf{Financing (seed)}: the base case is carried by the round alone, but the conservative case
needs a seed round: at its 100-paying-company trigger (\cf{conservative.date_to_100}) about €4k of
cash is left and cash turns negative two months later & The seed raise starts at 100 paying
companies (\cf{base.date_to_100} in the base case), or four months before the conservative trigger
if the pilot tracks the conservative case; the Vest Ventures Seed stage is one route. No seed amount
or valuation has been discussed yet. Levers not modelled: the co-founders stop drawing their salaries
again (about two more months); the paid-ads budget may be cut, and its effect on runway can be tested
in the workbook. \\
\textbf{Single market}: Romania only; REGES is Romania-specific; the UI is in Romanian & Romanian
compliance depth is the defensible core. Expansion to neighbouring countries (Moldova, Serbia,
Bulgaria, Hungary) is a hypothesis for after the seed (Section~\ref{sec:international}), not part of
this plan's numbers. \\
\textbf{IP assignment}: the code was written by the co-founders and is not yet assigned to the company
in writing & A written IP assignment from both co-founders to the SRL, signed before the round closes, as
part of the round's legal work; Răzvan Pervulescu enters the share capital at the same time. \\
\textbf{Competition}: accountant-owned payroll tools (SmartBill, SAGA, Nexus) and cheap time-tracking
apps & We do not compete on payroll; we serve the employer's daily HR work and give the accountant
exports and a login. Flat price per company is simple for micro-firms. \\
\textbf{Founder time}: both co-founders are students & Full-time commitment from the close is a
condition of the plan; they are unpaid in year~1, live on personal savings, and are paid from
month 13. \\
\textbf{Key person}: two founders hold all product knowledge & Developer hired after the seed (month 25 in the base case); code,
migrations and architecture are documented in the repository; CI and tests guard changes. \\
\textbf{Pricing ceiling}: flat price, no published price above 20 employees & A price for larger
companies is to be defined; the model understates revenue from them. \\
\textbf{Security and infrastructure}: HR data is sensitive; one database project serves dev and prod;
the AI assistant depends on an external model & Forced RLS, encryption, audit log; separate production
database, external pentest and key-custody procedure funded; the assistant is optional and executes nothing. \\
\textbf{Timing}: the Cohort 3 date is unknown & The model starts at the close; a later close shifts the
timeline but not the runway. \\
